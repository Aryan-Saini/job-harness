# The note to a YC founder

On Work at a Startup the note IS the application. A founder reads it in ten seconds between other
things. It should read like a short message from a real engineer who looked at their product, not
like a cover letter and not like AI.

## What it says (3 to 5 sentences, about 60 to 110 words)
1. Open on their product. Something specific from the posting or the company's one-liner: what
   they're building and an honest reaction to it, like what's hard or interesting about it, or why
   it clicked for the applicant. No "I'm excited to apply", no "I came across your posting".
2. Connect ONE piece of the applicant's real work to their problem, told plainly as what they built
   and why it's close to what the company does. Pick the closest match from context.md and the
   resume: a marketplace, a payments flow, a mobile app, an AI tool, an infrastructure migration.
   One thing, not a list.
3. End simply: what they'd want to dig into first, or a real question about the product.

## What it never does
- No dollar figures or stat dumps. One number only if it's the whole point, and usually it isn't.
- No resume recap, no list of technologies, no "I believe I'd be a great fit", "passionate",
  "leverage", "excited", "thrilled", "innovative", "cutting-edge", "I'd love the opportunity".
- No em dashes, no semicolons, no rule-of-three lists, no "not just X but Y", no sign-off like
  "Looking forward to hearing from you" or "Best, <name>".
- Never invents anything about the applicant or about the company. If the posting says little about
  the product, react to what it does say.

## How it sounds
The applicant's voice from context.md, written properly: first person, contractions, normal
capitalization. Sentences of different lengths, glued with "and" and "so" rather than
"additionally". Direct, a bit casual, curious about the product. Like a text to a founder they respect.

Bad (robotic): "I'm excited to apply for the Founding Engineer role. I grew revenue 300% and led a
team of 5, and I'm passionate about innovative solutions."

Good (shape only, never copy it): "Routing jobs to the right crew in real time is a fun problem and
it's close to what I work on now. I built the dispatch for a local services app that offers each job
to nearby workers one at a time, so I've hit a lot of the edge cases already. I'd want to start on how
you match supply when a market is thin."
